3. HIG review and fixes

Reviewed with the apple-look skill against Apple's HIG pages for accessibility, color, dark mode, typography, sidebars, the menu bar, popovers, charts, materials and motion. Rating before fixes: Good, with two High findings. Both are fixed below. What already held up: system font and semantic label colours in both appearances, sidebar icons in the accent colour, a single accent for actions, spring motion anchored to its source and turned off under Reduce Motion, solid surfaces under Reduce Transparency, and stronger labels under Increase Contrast.

High

DarkWake bands drowned the battery line

62 full-height orange bars at 62% opacity filled most of the chart, so the line people came for was hard to follow. charts.md › Best practices: “Too many grid lines can be visually overwhelming, distracting people from the data.” The same goes for bands.

DarkWake bands dropped to 20% opacity, and each wake also gets a solid tick in a thin lane along the bottom. The line reads first, the wakes second.

High

Awake and DarkWake told apart by colour only

Teal and orange bands of similar width. accessibility.md › Color and effects: “Convey information with more than color alone.” charts.md: “Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate…”

The awake band now carries a text label on the chart, DarkWakes have their own tick lane (shape, not just hue), and the legend swatch for DarkWake is striped.

Medium

Measured / estimated badges sat at the 10 pt floor

Badges were 0.78 em of 13 pt, about 10.1 pt. accessibility.md › Text size: macOS minimum is 10 pt. Too close for the one label the whole app's honesty depends on.

Badges raised to 0.85 em (11 pt). Contrast of badge text: light 6.2:1 on white, dark 7.4:1 on the window colour.

Medium

Chart labels did not scale with text size

The 70% and 80% grid labels were drawn inside the SVG, so they stayed small with Large text on. accessibility.md › Text size: support larger text in every part of the interface.

Grid labels and the Awake label moved into text placed over the chart, so they follow the system text size like the axis below.

Medium

Sidebar could not be hidden

sidebars.md › Best practices: “Consider letting people hide the sidebar.”

Added the standard sidebar toggle beside the window controls (NavigationSplitView gives this for free). The sidebar also collapses to icons when the window gets narrow.

Medium

Pre-calibration baseline row was labelled Estimated

A row saying ‘Baseline not measured yet’ with an Estimated badge contradicts itself. writing.md: one meaning per label.

New dashed ‘Not measured’ badge, value shown as a dash, and that energy is counted under Unexplained with a note saying so.

Medium

Menu bar strip too tall, icon unclear

the-menu-bar.md › Menu bar extras: “The menu bar’s height is 24 pt.” The strip was 27 pt and scaled with text size; the battery-with-bolt icon read as Low Power Mode.

Menu bar fixed at 24 pt with 16 pt template icons. The Drainlog extra is now a battery outline with a falling line inside, monochrome so the system can tint it.

Low

Legend spacing and the Total row

‘52 min , 62 wakes’ had a stray gap, and the Total row said ‘14 pts’, a unit used nowhere else.

Legend reads ‘DarkWake 52 min in 62 wakes’. Total share column now says 100%.

Medium

A popover where HIG prefers a menu

the-menu-bar.md › Menu bar extras: “Display a menu — not a popover — when people click your menu bar extra. Unless the app functionality you want to expose is too complex for a menu…”

A timeline chart cannot live in an NSMenu, so this uses MenuBarExtra with the window style. It closes on click outside, has no Close button (popovers.md › Best practices), and the last row behaves like a menu item with ⌘O.

Low

White on system blue is 4.0:1

The Open Battery Settings button is white on #007AFF, under the 4.5:1 in accessibility.md for 13 pt text.

It is the system prominent button and will be built with .borderedProminent, so macOS draws it, follows the user's accent colour and darkens it under Increase Contrast. Not restyled.

For the build: every toolbar item also gets a menu command (Go › Previous Night ⌘[, Next Night ⌘]), and the chart gets an accessibility chart descriptor so VoiceOver can read it as an audio graph.

Approve or request changes

Once these are approved, the SwiftUI shell gets built to match. Useful things to react to:

  • The headline wording and whether 14% or 520 mAh should lead.
  • The band colours: indigo for asleep, orange for DarkWake, teal for awake.
  • How measured, estimated (≈) and unexplained (?) are marked.
  • What the popover leaves out compared with the window.
